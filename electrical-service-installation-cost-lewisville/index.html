Scope, timing, cost factors, and terms

A service installation discussion may cover the connection point, service conductors, meter equipment, the main panel, and any related work. What applies depends on the property and the requested job. Some projects may also involve coordination or approval steps. Ask who is responsible for confirming those requirements before work begins.

Key facts to establish:

  • What it covers: the exact equipment and labor included, plus any related repairs or exclusions.
  • Typical timing: evaluation, estimate preparation, scheduling, installation, and any required review happen in stages. The calendar time depends on the job, access, materials, and approvals, so ask for a project-specific schedule.
  • What affects cost: equipment choices, the amount of wiring or replacement work, access to the work area, existing conditions, and any additional tasks found during assessment.

When comparing providers, ask each one to describe the same scope. Check whether the estimate lists materials, labor, possible extra work, and responsibility for permits or inspections if those apply. Ask how a change in scope would be discussed and approved. Don’t compare only the bottom-line total if the included work differs.

A few terms you may hear: service entrance means the conductors and related equipment bringing electrical service into a building; meter base is the equipment that holds the electric meter; panel is where circuits are distributed and protected. Ask the electrician to point out which parts are part of your project.
